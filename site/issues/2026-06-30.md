## The Front Page

### Oxford Nanoimaging launches Aplo Flow for automated super-resolution workflows

- 30 June 2026 | Sources: [Oxford Nanoimaging](https://oni.bio/news/aplo-flow-expands-automation-for-complex-imaging-workflows/) and [ONI LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/onibio_superresolution-spatialbiology-microscopy-activity-7477617223872778240-lBT9)
- Oxford Nanoimaging announced Aplo Flow, an integrated fluidics automation system for the Aplo Platform.
- The company says Aplo Flow automates multiplexed fluid exchange for live-cell assays and imaging, including drug-delivery, particle-uptake, DNA-PAINT, RESI and MERFISH workflows, with reproducible 15 nm-resolution experiments at scale.
- ONI also claims the system can run up to four samples in parallel and preserved more than 99% of spatial data across multiple imaging cycles and fields of view, with negligible signal carryover. Those are company-reported performance claims; no independent validation was identified in-window.
- Why it matters: this is a real product-extension announcement rather than conference marketing. It moves ONI's super-resolution story toward workflow automation, throughput and reproducibility, which are practical adoption bottlenecks in spatial biology and therapeutic nanoparticle characterization. Confidence: High on launch and company-stated specifications; medium on performance implications pending user data.

### Seloxium wins European Innovation Council support for SELECTAL critical-metals recovery

- 23 June 2026 | Sources: [Seloxium](https://www.seloxium.com/seloxium-awarded-grant-from-the-european-council-to-transform-industrial-waste-into-valuable-resources/) and [Seloxium news index](https://www.seloxium.com/news/)
- Seloxium said it has been awarded European Innovation Council funding for SELECTAL, its project for scalable, selective and low-cost recovery of critical metals from complex waste.
- The company describes SELECTAL as a liquid metal-binding polymer platform for recovering materials including rhodium, palladium, gold and rare earth elements from dilute or complex chemical and mining waste streams.
- Seloxium says the platform can achieve over 96% recovery rates and over 99% purity, and that EIC support will help advance commercialisation of modular systems deployable at the waste source. Those technical and deployment claims remain company-reported.
- Why it matters: Seloxium now has a second June policy/funding signal after appearing in UK critical-minerals coverage last week. The strategic theme is consistent: domestic and European supply-chain resilience is looking for technologies that recover critical materials from waste rather than new primary extraction alone. Confidence: High on company-reported award; medium on programme size and commercial timetable until EIC project-level documentation is available.

## Specialist Desks

### Mixergy takes Unlock Net Zero innovation prize for St Basils smart-hot-water pilot

- 24 June 2026 | Sources: [Mixergy](https://www.mixergy.co.uk/blog/mixergy-wins-innovation-of-the-year-at-the-unlock-net-zero-awards/) and [Unlock Net Zero Awards shortlist](https://www.unlocknetzero.co.uk/awards-shortlist/shortlist-unzla)
- Mixergy said it won Innovation of the Year at the Unlock Net Zero Awards on 23 June for work with youth homelessness charity St Basils.
- The company says the pilot combines smart hot water, heating, ventilation and energy-use control, with early testing showing roughly 30% lower energy use and around GBP 200 annual resident savings.
- The award is not equivalent to a new financing or commercial deployment contract, but the underlying pilot is strategically relevant because it places Mixergy's technology in social-housing retrofit, affordability and healthy-homes use cases.
- Why it matters: housing retrofit is a difficult market where payback, resident comfort and compliance all matter. A measured pilot with a named charity partner is more substantive than a generic award mention, though wider rollout and independently audited results remain follow-up questions. Confidence: Medium-High.

### Prolific case study links its annotator pool to Ai2's robotics-video benchmark work

- 23 June 2026 | Sources: [Prolific](https://www.prolific.com/resources/how-ai2-built-a-new-benchmark-for-robotics-videos-with-prolific-s-expert-annotators) and [Ai2](https://allenai.org/)
- Prolific published a case study on how the Allen Institute for AI used Prolific annotators for Molmo2 video-understanding work and a real-world robotics-video benchmark.
- The case study says Ai2 used Prolific's dedicated video annotator pool for object-tracking tasks involving videos, object selection, text labels, frame-by-frame tracking and occlusion marking.
- Prolific frames the work as a quality and scale story for human annotation in multimodal AI, rather than a new customer announcement with disclosed contract terms.
- Why it matters: Prolific's core strategic value is becoming clearer in the AI evaluation and training stack: expert human data for perception-heavy tasks that models cannot reliably self-label. The customer is named and the use case is concrete, but the evidence is a company case study. Confidence: Medium.
